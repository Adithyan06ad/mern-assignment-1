The Starry Night

Artist Name Vincent van Gogh
Year of Creation 1889
Medium Oil on canvas
Dimensions 73.7 cm × 92.1 cm
Current Repository The Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York
Movement / Period Post-Impressionism

Curatorial Analysis

Painted in June 1889 from the east-facing window of Vincent van Gogh's room in the Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum in Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, The Starry Night stands as one of Western art’s most celebrated achievements. The painting features an intensely turbulent, swirling night sky punctuated by eleven radiant stars and a blazing crescent moon.

In the foreground, a dark, flame-like cypress tree rises dramatically, acting as a symbolic conduit between the terrestrial village and the cosmic infinity above. Van Gogh’s rhythmic impasto technique and vibrant juxtaposition of cobalt blues and cadmium yellows render emotional vitality rather than literal observation.

“Looking at the stars always makes me dream, as simply as I dream over the black dots representing towns and villages on a map.” — Vincent van Gogh, Letter to Theo (1889)
