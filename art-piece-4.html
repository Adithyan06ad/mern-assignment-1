The Kiss (Der Kuss)

Artist Name Gustav Klimt
Year of Creation 1907 – 1908
Medium Oil and gold leaf on canvas
Dimensions 180 cm × 180 cm (Square)
Current Repository Österreichische Galerie Belvedere, Vienna
Movement / Period Vienna Secession • Art Nouveau

Curatorial Analysis

Created during the zenith of Gustav Klimt’s famed “Golden Phase”, The Kiss is an opulent square canvas depicting two lovers locked in an impassioned, sacred embrace on a wildflower meadow verge. The figures are enveloped in magnificent ceremonial robes crafted from shimmering gold leaf, silver, and intricate geometric ornamentation.

Klimt was deeply moved by the shimmering early Christian Byzantine mosaics of Ravenna, which he witnessed firsthand during his 1903 travels in Italy. In the painting, the man’s robe features masculine, rigid rectangular geometries in black and white, while the woman’s gown is adorned with organic concentric circular spirals and floral ripples, synthesizing human intimacy into eternal cosmic harmony.

“Whoever wants to know something about me as an artist ought to look carefully at my pictures and try to see in them what I am and what I want to do.” — Gustav Klimt, Commentary on an Unwritten Self-Portrait
