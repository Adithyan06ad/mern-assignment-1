Mona Lisa (La Gioconda)

Artist Name Leonardo da Vinci
Year of Creation 1503 (Begun)
Medium Oil on Lombardy poplar panel
Dimensions 77 cm × 53 cm
Current Repository Musée du Louvre, Salle des États, Paris
Movement / Period Italian High Renaissance

Curatorial Analysis

Commenced in Florence around 1503, Leonardo da Vinci's portrait of Lisa Gherardini, wife of silk merchant Francesco del Giocondo, stands as the most visited, analyzed, and legendary painting on earth. Leonardo maintained this panel with him until his passing at Clos Lucé in France, applying dozens of microscopic glaze layers.

The painting revolutionized portraiture through the mastery of sfumato—the imperceptible blending of color and tone without crisp borders or sharp outlines, creating an illusion of living, breathing flesh. The subject’s subtle, shifting smile is mirrored by the fantastical aerial landscape in the background, featuring winding paths, rivers, and glacial rock formations that evoke the primordial forces of nature.

“The eye sees a thing more clearly in dreams than the imagination awake... Art is never finished, only abandoned.” — Leonardo da Vinci, Notebooks
