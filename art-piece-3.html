The Great Wave off Kanagawa

Artist Name Katsushika Hokusai (葛飾 北斎)
Year of Creation 1831 (Edo Period)
Medium Color woodblock print (Nishiki-e)
Dimensions 25.7 cm × 37.8 cm
Current Repository Tokyo National Museum / British Museum
Movement / Period Ukiyo-e • Edo Period

Curatorial Analysis

Published in 1831 as the inaugural print in Katsushika Hokusai's landmark series Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji, The Great Wave off Kanagawa (神奈川沖浪裏) remains the most reproduced Japanese artwork in human history. The print dramatizes three oshiokuri-bune fast cargo boats pitching desperately through colossal cresting waves.

Hokusai utilizes deep synthetic Prussian blue ink (newly imported through Nagasaki trading routes) alongside indigo and white foam claws. In the deep background, the sacred Mount Fuji sits quiet and diminutive, juxtaposing nature’s fleeting tempestuous rage against geological eternity. This woodblock dramatically influenced European Impressionism and Japonisme across Paris.

“From the age of six I had a penchant for copying the form of things... yet by the age of eighty-six I shall have penetrated to the mystery of things themselves.” — Katsushika Hokusai, Preface to 100 Views of Mount Fuji
